A gladiator fights, trains, and eventually retires. Champions are meant to end — the academy they leave behind is what lasts.

The loop

Fight a run in the arena for gold and renown, then train through the cooldown, spend what you earned, and go again. A run is five to twelve minutes; training runs for hours while the tab is closed.

Training

Pick a facility and leave it. Training banks TP the whole time you are away, up to eight hours, and the longer one session runs the better the rate — so checking in less often is rewarded, not punished. Press Collect to turn banked TP into stat and weapon levels. The Medic heals instead of teaching.

The arena

Rounds get harder and pay more, forever. You choose when to walk: yielding keeps 70% of the pot and your health, a knockout keeps the whole pot but costs you 25 minutes on the bench — during which you cannot train or heal either. The run keeps fighting while the tab is closed.

The sand, and the boons

Two conditions are in force at any moment — shown on the Arena screen before you enter, and they change every twenty minutes. Each has an upside and a cost: a rich purse with hungrier opponents, weaker enemies and a thinner purse. A run keeps whatever sand it started on, so entering is a real choice: fight this one, or wait for sand that suits what you are carrying.

Every opponent has an archetype — a Brawler hits far harder than it can take, a Turtle is a long way from falling over, a Swarmer strikes twice for half as much, and a Venomous one leaves wounds that keep working after the exchange. It is named above their health bar, and it is the reason one round plays differently from the next. Some of them can put a status on you, which is new: bleed and stun used to be things only you did to them.

Every tenth round is an elite — a named challenger with a forced archetype, more health, and a purse worth two and a half rounds.

The crowd meter fills a full notch for every round you clear, and a fraction of one every time you land a critical hit or slip a blow — so a flashy fighter wins it over sooner. Held full it pays +15% gold every round; spent, it either recovers some health or takes a large bite out of whoever you are facing. Holding it is the default, and you can set what happens while you are away under the arena card.

Every five rounds you are offered three boons and take one. They last for that run only, they stack, and the fight does not stop while you choose — the boon starts working the moment you take it. If you are not watching, the game takes one for you after twelve exchanges, using the preference you set under the arena card.

A deep start does not cost you boons. If you have Seeded Entry and begin at round 24, you are owed every boon a run that fought its way there would have — four of them, all offered before the first exchange.

Gear and augments

Quality tiers sit on the slot, so switching weapon type costs you nothing. Better quality opens more augment slots — one at Fine, two at Master, three at Legendary, on each piece.

There are two separate augment pools and they do not mix. Weapon augments are what you do to the enemy: crit, crit damage, raw damage, affliction, gold, lifesteal, and one that speeds weapon training. Armour augments are what the arena does to you: evasion, damage reduction, max HP, in-fight regen, out-of-combat healing speed, and a shorter knockout bench.

Fitting and removing are both free, so a build is never locked in — only the gold you spent buying an augment is gone.

Two things are easy to get wrong. Vampiric and Resilient share one healing budget, so owning both is owning one — the shop says so on the row. And Honed pays nothing in the arena; it speeds weapon training only, which is a real reason to buy it and not a combat one.

The sustain cap — read this before you stack healing

Everything that heals you mid-fight — lifesteal from a class or Vampiric, and regen from Resilient — draws from one shared budget, capped each exchange at 0.08% of your max HP.

Because the cap is a share of your health pool rather than of your damage, lifesteal saturates it almost immediately. The rule:

If you hit for more than 1% of your own max HP, 8% lifesteal is already at the cap. Everything above that heals you for nothing.

What that means in practice:

  • Take one sustain source, never two. A lifesteal class or Vampiric or Resilient. A second one adds exactly zero — not less, zero.
  • Resilient fills the whole budget by itself at any damage level. Pair it with a lifesteal class and the class's lifesteal is completely wasted.
  • To heal more, raise max HP, not lifesteal. The cap scales with your health pool, so CON and +max HP bonuses lift it. The lifesteal percentage on a class card does not.
  • The exception is a slow tank — very high CON, low damage. If your hit is under 1% of your max HP, extra lifesteal genuinely scales for you.

One sustain source returns roughly a third of your health pool over a full run, which is well worth a slot. The second returns none of it — spend that slot on gold, cooldown or damage instead.

Battle classes

Earned by weapon proficiency, not bought. Each grants two or three bonuses and some carry a drawback in exchange for stronger ones. A class gains levels 1–4 as you clear rounds wearing it, and its bonuses grow with the level.

Renown and rank

Renown is fame, and it only ever goes up. Each rank gives your champion a new title and a small permanent bonus, and the highest rank you reach raises the floor every future champion starts from.

Stories

Each champion answers for one tale — the one the academy is in the middle of. The rest of the library stays shut until they have finished that tale themselves; a tale a predecessor closed is not one you have told.

Chapters gate on your own best round, not the academy's. Closed chapters, and the name of whoever closed them, are permanent — so a successor picks up a half-told tale where it was left, but still has to earn their way to the next boss.

Retirement

Retiring is not losing. Your champion joins the Hall, pays out Legacy Points for permanent account perks, and can be seated as a mentor to speed the training of everyone after them. Mentors wear out, so you always need the next champion.

Daily commission

One small task a day, on the Arena screen. Completing it pays gold, and consecutive days pay more — up to five. Miss a day and the streak starts over.
